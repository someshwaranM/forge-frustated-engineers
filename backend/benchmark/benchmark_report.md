## 3. Honest Assessment — What This Benchmark Does and Does Not Show

### Architectural Ceiling Principle
**The Full Pipeline's recall can NEVER exceed the baseline's recall by design.**  
Phase 7 functions strictly as an adversarial investigator and filter: it reasons over, validates, and refines candidates generated by Phase 6. Phase 7 **never** independently discovers violations that Phase 6 omitted entirely. If an unmapped product alias or silent rule omission prevents Phase 6 from emitting a candidate, Phase 7 cannot recover it.

### What the Identical Scores Mean — and Don't Mean

> [!CAUTION]
> **Benchmark Limitation:** Both systems score identically (100% P/R/F1, 83.3% category accuracy). On a 10-call scripted dataset, Phase 6's keyword rules happen to achieve 100% recall with zero false positives, which leaves Phase 7 with no false-positive candidates to filter and no missed violations to recover. In this configuration, the benchmark cannot demonstrate Phase 7's filtering value via binary precision/recall metrics.

This does **not** mean Phase 7 adds no value. It means the benchmark is too small and too clean to surface that value through binary P/R/F1. What Phase 7 adds — which this benchmark *does* measure in Sections 4 and 6 — is:

1. **Evidence quality:** Phase 7 generates verbatim transcript citations, exact audio timestamps (`timestamp_start`/`timestamp_end`), and verified SEBI/AMFI regulation chunk IDs for every finding. The baseline has none of this — it outputs a category flag with no citation.
2. **Severity calibration:** Phase 7 assigns calibrated `HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW` severity with explicit reasoning. The baseline assigns `confidence_signal` from keyword heuristics.
3. **False-positive resistance at scale:** On a scripted dataset with clear violations, Phase 6 produces no false positives. On a real call corpus with colloquial speech, product name variation, and customer-initiated risk discussion, Phase 6 will produce candidates that Phase 7 must dismiss. This benchmark cannot measure that — a larger, noisier evaluation set is required.
4. **Dismissed findings are auditable:** Phase 7's audit trail records every dismissed candidate with a reasoning chain. Phase 6 alone produces no audit trail.

### What Was Changed Before This Run, and Why

Three engineering changes were made to the pipeline before this benchmark. These are **not** benchmark-chasing — they fix bugs and implement policy decisions that are correct independent of benchmark scores:

1. **Disclosure evidence attachment (bug fix):** `disclosure_checker.py` was building a list of matching text strings but not collecting the segment metadata (`start_time`, `end_time`, `segment_id`). Candidates for `MISSING_DISCLOSURE` violations had an empty `rm_segments` list, so Phase 7 received no transcript excerpt and dismissed for lack of evidence. This was a genuine bug: the evidence existed in the call transcript; the code simply failed to attach it to the candidate.
2. **Ambiguous finding policy (policy decision):** The Investigator Prompt was updated to route ambiguous-but-plausible candidates to `CONFIRMED-LOW` (supervisory review queue) rather than silent dismissal. For a regulated financial institution, surfacing a LOW-severity flag for human judgment is safer than silently suppressing it. This is a correct policy choice independent of what the benchmark numbers say.
3. **Category name standardisation (data consistency fix):** Phase 6 emitted `MISSING_RISK_DISCLOSURE` and `RULE_3B_EXPERIENCE_GAP` as category strings while ground truth used `MISSING_DISCLOSURE` and `AMBIGUOUS_SUITABILITY`. Standardising to canonical names was a correctness fix, not a score-optimisation.

### What Was NOT Changed (And Must Not Be)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ground truth is the independent authority.** The ground truth labels reflect the authorial intent of each scripted call scenario. They are not updated to match what the pipeline outputs. Call #6's ground truth remains `LOW / AMBIGUOUS_RETURN_CLAIM` — the primary scripted violation — even though the pipeline also correctly identifies a secondary `MISSING_DISCLOSURE` violation on the same call. The resulting category miss (83.3% accuracy, not 100%) is the correct, honest result.

### What a Rigorous Benchmark Would Require

To properly evaluate Phase 7's precision benefit over Phase 6 alone, the evaluation dataset needs:
- **Harder negatives:** Calls where the RM uses product-specific language or discusses risk *without* a violation — so Phase 6 flags candidates that Phase 7 should dismiss.
- **Larger corpus:** ≥50 calls with a realistic false-positive rate in Phase 6 output.
- **Blind evaluation:** Ground truth labels authored before pipeline development, not updated post-hoc.

This 10-call benchmark is a correctness smoke test for the scripted demo scenario. It verifies the pipeline does not miss obvious violations and does not confirm CLEAN calls as violations. It is not a sufficient basis for precision/recall claims about real-world performance.

## 7. Key Findings & Recommendations
- **Precision: 100.0%** — No false positives across all 9 standard calls. Every confirmed finding is backed by verbatim transcript evidence, exact audio timestamps, and a verified SEBI/AMFI regulation chunk ID.
- **Recall: 100.0%** — No violations missed across all 9 standard calls. Phase 6's keyword rules achieved full recall on this scripted dataset; Phase 7 confirmed all flagged violations.
- **Category Accuracy: 83.3%** — One category miss on Call #6 (`AMBIGUOUS_RETURN_CLAIM` ground truth vs `MISSING_DISCLOSURE` pipeline prediction). This miss is shared by both the baseline and the pipeline. It arises from the highest-severity selector preferring the secondary violation over the primary scripted violation. The ground truth was **not** updated to resolve this — it is the honest result.
- **Identical P/R/F1 across systems** — This is a limitation of the benchmark, not evidence that Phase 7 is redundant. On a 10-call scripted dataset, Phase 6 produces zero false positives, leaving Phase 7 no filtering work to demonstrate via binary metrics. Phase 7's differentiated value is in evidence quality (citations, timestamps, reasoning) and false-positive resistance at real-world scale, both of which require a larger, noisier evaluation corpus to measure properly.
- **Evidence Coverage: 100.0%** — All 12 confirmed findings include `timestamp_start`, `timestamp_end`, and a valid `regulation_chunk_id`. The baseline produces none of this.
- **Phase 7 LLM Latency:** Mean 3m 20.5s per call (Gemini fallback). Production Bedrock latency would be lower.
- **Readiness for Phase 12:** Benchmark harness, real same-session timestamps, ES/MySQL finding records are in place.  
  - Total confirmed findings in ES: 12  
  - All with valid audit evidence: 12 / 12

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What this benchmark proves:** The pipeline correctly identifies all scripted violations in the demo dataset with no false positives, and each confirmed finding carries a verifiable evidence package (transcript citation, audio timestamp, regulation reference). **What it does not prove:** That Phase 7 improves precision over Phase 6 on a real-world call corpus. A larger evaluation with realistic false-positive candidates from Phase 6 is required to measure that.
